Research interests

My work is in computational topology. Its common thread is the use of combinatorial structures to describe and compute the shape of a space, whether it is a simplicial complex, an image or a dataset. My main research programme concerns multiparameter discrete Morse theory and my postdoctoral work applies geometric and topological methods to machine learning.

Multiparameter discrete Morse theory

From real-valued to vector-valued functions

Morse theory studies how the topology of a space changes as one sweeps through the sublevel sets of a real-valued function. It thus links each critical point of the function to a homological feature of the space. More precisely, each singularity corresponds to the appearance or disappearance of a connected component, a hole or a void. Discrete Morse theory, introduced by Forman, carries these ideas to a combinatorial setting that is well suited to computation.

During my PhD, under the supervision of Tomasz Kaczynski and Madjid Allili, I extended the theory of Forman to functions with values in ℝk, which may be used to filter a space with several parameters simultaneously. In this theory, called multiparameter discrete Morse (MDM) theory, the critical cells of a function are not isolated, unlike in the original framework. Instead, they group into critical components, analogous to the Pareto sets of smooth vector-valued functions. The central results of Forman, notably the Morse inequalities, extend to this setting (Brouillette, Allili and Kaczynski, 2024).

Computing multiparameter persistent homology

MDM theory is closely linked to multiparameter persistent homology, whose efficient computation presents a challenge. The algorithm GenerateMDM takes as input any multifiltering function defined on a simplicial complex and outputs a MDM function which approximates it and whose gradient field is compatible with the induced multifiltration (Brouillette, 2025). The complex can then be reduced to its critical cells without affecting its multiparameter persistent homology. The same paper defines the discrete Pareto set of a multifiltering function and compares its connected components with the critical components of the resulting MDM function. An interactive applet runs the algorithm on the examples of the paper.

Applications to machine learning

Reconstruction of fragmented objects

Reassembling a broken object from its fragments is a recurring problem in archaeology and forensics. A key step is to determine whether two fragments are adjacent and, if so, to locate their shared boundary.

As part of my postdoctoral work at UQTR with Alain Goupil, Pierre-Olivier Parisé and Fadel Touré, I develop deep learning models that predict the adjacency of 2D fragments from their contours. Our preprint proposes a descriptor based on the tangent angle along the contour, which is invariant under rotation and independent of the starting point of the contour. It also proposes a band U-Net that segments the shared boundary in the adjacency matrix of a pair of fragments.

Relevance and diversity in LLMs

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) provides a large language model with passages taken from a corpus, selected according to the proximity of their embeddings to that of the query. However, when the corpus is redundant, the nearest neighbours of the query tend to repeat the same information.

This idea was studied during a Mitacs Accelerate project at UQTR with Faustin Kagabo, Usef Faghihi and Nadia Ghazzali. Our preprint shows that diversifying the retrieved passages helps or hurts depending on their redundancy. It proposes an adaptive rule that diversifies only when needed as well as the RNG-Score, a geometric reranking method (code, applet).

Earlier work

Multicomplex fractals

Multicomplex numbers generalize complex numbers to dimensions 4, 8, 16 and beyond. The classic Mandelbrot and Julia sets extend naturally to the induced spaces in which they can be visualized through their three-dimensional slices.

In my master's thesis, supervised by Dominic Rochon, I classified the principal 3D slices of the multicomplex Multibrot sets using tools from dynamical systems, algebra and geometry (Brouillette and Rochon, 2019). With Pierre-Olivier Parisé and Dominic Rochon, I also obtained distance estimates for tricomplex filled-in Julia sets and Multibrot sets (Brouillette, Parisé and Rochon, 2019).

Polyominoes

During undergraduate research internships supervised by Alain Goupil, I studied and enumerated various families of polyominoes, which introduced me to combinatorics and discrete geometry.
